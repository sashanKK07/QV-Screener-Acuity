01 The screener

Find businesses that are both high quality and cheap, judged against their own sector

The screen runs on ARCUS, the SEC ingestion layer I built earlier. It pulls XBRL financials straight from EDGAR, rebuilds point-in-time statements, and traces every number back to the filing it came from. The fundamentals come from the filings themselves, not a data vendor, so every input can be checked.

Quality · pillar 1
Is capital used with discipline?
Management efficiency: asset growth, share issuance, leverage
Quality · pillar 2
Are returns high and steady?
Profitability: gross profitability, ROIC, stability of ROE
Quality · pillar 3
Do earnings turn into cash?
Cash flow: free cash flow, accruals
Value
What am I paying for it?
Earnings, free-cash-flow and EBITDA yields; book to price

Every metric is ranked within its sector, so a software company is never compared with a utility. A stock has to be strong on both quality and value, and stay near the top however the two are weighted. Piotroski F, Beneish M and Altman Z sit beside every name as red-flag checks, shown but never scored. The pillar structure follows Northern Trust’s published quality research; it is my approximation, not their model.

04 Ruling names out

Four of the top five, and why the filings sent them home

Ranked #1 · Dropbox (DBX)

−$2.2Bbook equity

  • Negative equity inflates ROIC. Years of buybacks pushed book equity below zero, so return on capital reads far higher than the business earns.
  • Free cash flow is flattered because equipment financed through leases never shows up as capex.
Ranked #2 · EnerSys (ENS)
Quality built on a tax credit with an end date

  • The Section 45X manufacturing credit was $158.6M in FY26, booked as lower cost of goods: about $4.15 of $10.56 adjusted EPS.
  • It phases down to 75% in 2030, 50% in 2031, 25% in 2032, then zero. Without it, I estimate ~15x EV/EBITDA vs 11x screened, and P/E ~34x vs ~18x.

05 Why Acuity

Priced like a lighting company. Becoming a building-technology company.

Acuity’s quality depends least on any single customer, product, subsidy or date, and its mix is shifting toward a higher-margin segment the market hasn’t re-priced.

■ Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL)

~$3.6B sales · 18.1% adj. margin

Commercial lighting (Lithonia, Juno, Holophane, Gotham) sold through specifiers, agents and distributors. Mature and tied to construction.

■ Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS)

~$1.1B sales · 23.0% adj. margin

Distech building controls, Atrius cloud software and QSC’s Q-SYS audio, video and control platform. Sold through integrators; sticky once installed.

07 The honest read

The market already prices most of the base case. The upside is the mix shift.

At $303 the stock sits just under my base case and the probability-weighted value is only ~3% higher. The return comes from Intelligent Spaces compounding toward the bull case, which is worth ~$392, not from the base case being mispriced.

What would prove me wrong
  • AIS organic growth below lighting for two straight quarters
  • AIS adjusted margin below 21%
  • An acquisition that takes net leverage above ~2x
Risks I’m carrying
  • The construction cycle: beta above 1 and lighting flat to down
  • Memory costs: a ~200 bp hit to AIS gross margin in FY27
  • Mexico makes 57% of finished goods: tariffs and labor
  • Capital deployment: management says it will deploy capital “aggressively”
